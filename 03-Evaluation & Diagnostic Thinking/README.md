# U3 — PROVE IT WORKS

## Quality Thinking

> Building a solution is not the same as proving it works.

In this unit, I move from building solutions to **testing their behavior and evaluating their quality through evidence**.

I learn to design clear test cases, compare expected and actual results, identify errors and unusual scenarios, investigate failures, and use findings to guide improvements.

The goal is not to prove that a system works once, but to develop a repeatable evaluation process that reveals its reliability and limitations.

---

## 🎯 Capability

**Quality Thinking**

The ability to assess system behavior and output quality through systematic testing, result analysis, failure diagnosis, and documented evidence.

---

## 🧠 What I Learned

- **Testing Strategy:** Define testing goals, scope, and success criteria.
- **Test Cases:** Design scenarios covering normal usage and unexpected conditions.
- **Expected vs. Actual Results:** Compare intended behavior with observed system outcomes.
- **Edge Cases:** Test unusual inputs and boundary conditions.
- **Failure Analysis:** Investigate the causes and impact of errors.
- **Iteration:** Refine solutions based on test findings and retest the changes.
- **Evidence-Based Evaluation:** Support conclusions with documented test results rather than assumptions.

## 🔍 Engineering Focus

Throughout this unit, I explore six practical engineering questions:

1. What behavior should the system deliver?
2. How can I design a test that verifies that behavior?
3. Which scenarios might expose hidden weaknesses?
4. Do actual results match expected results?
5. What explains the failures I observe?
6. How can I verify that a change produced a measurable improvement?

---

## 📌 Key Takeaway

**A working demonstration is useful. Repeatable tests and documented evidence provide stronger confidence.**

One successful demonstration is not enough to establish quality. Repeatable tests, failure analysis, and documented evidence help produce more reliable solutions and better-informed engineering decisions.
